About

A brief introduction

I am a doctoral researcher in Statistics at the Indian Statistical Institute, Kolkata, advised by Prof. Anil K. Ghosh. My work develops statistical methods for data whose dimension is large, often far larger than the sample size. A central theme is change‑point detection: finding when the distribution of a high‑dimensional sequence shifts, using distance‑based and graph‑based statistics that make few assumptions about the data. Closely related threads include nonparametric two‑sample testing, high‑dimensional asymptotics, and limit theorems for statistics on random graphs and sampled networks.

Before the PhD, I completed my M.Stat (Computational Statistics) at ISI Kolkata and a B.Sc. in Statistics at Presidency University, Kolkata.

Beyond research, I practise Indian classical flute and harmonica, and draw quiet inspiration from Vedic philosophical traditions that shape both my outlook on research and daily life. I write about these intersections on my blog.

Research

Areas of inquiry

My work sits at the interface of high-dimensional theory and data-driven methodology, where rigorous mathematics serves practical inference.

§ i

Change-Point Detection

Nonparametric, distance-based methods for detecting abrupt distributional shifts in multivariate and high-dimensional data streams, with provable guarantees.

Pairwise distancesBinary segmentation
§ ii

Nonparametric Inference & Two-Sample Testing

Distance-based and graph-based tests for comparing multivariate distributions, with exact or permutation calibration and consistency in high dimensions.

Two-sample testsGraph-based inference
§ iii

High-Dimensional Statistics

Inference when the dimension grows with, or far exceeds, the sample size: asymptotic behaviour of distance-based statistics and HDLSS regimes.

HDLSS asymptoticsDistance concentration
§ iv

Random Graph Models

Limit theorems for subgraph and edge statistics in random graphs and sampled networks, with applications to motif estimation and graph-based tests.

Motif estimationNetwork inference
